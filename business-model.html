The Company Behind PROOF

We're not selling a hiring tool.
We're building the signal layer for human work.

PROOF is the product. NBL is the company. The product gets us to product-market fit. The company gets us to a billion. They are not the same bet.

The co-founder thesis: The resume is a 50-year-old protocol. Every protocol gets replaced eventually — and whoever owns the replacement becomes infrastructure. Most companies attacking hiring try to be a better product. We are building the better protocol. Act 1 — we ship a consumer-led product that earns trust and generates the first scored work artifacts in the world. Act 2 — we license the signal back to every hiring system on earth.

Three shapes we considered

Same insight. Three radically different companies.

Before committing to a model, we mapped the three credible shapes this company could take. Each has a different customer, a different defensibility curve, and a different exit. The product matters less than the shape.

The co-founder decision

We don't pick one. We stack two. Option 2 is the wedge — it's the only shape that generates the proprietary scored-work dataset nobody else has, because nobody else is foolish enough to run a consumer product in this category. That dataset is the fuel for Option 1, which is where the durable billion-dollar business lives. Option 3 we build last, on top of the data we'll own by then. One company, two acts, three monetisation surfaces.

The moat

Why this becomes uncopyable, fast.

Every hiring company has tried to be a better resume. None have the courage to be a different protocol — because the protocol play requires a multi-year unprofitable data accumulation phase. That's our moat: nobody else will pay the cost.

Four moats stacking on each other.
A single moat is fragile. Four moats compounding makes this a fortress. Each one strengthens as the others grow. By year 4, the company is unattackable in this category.
MOAT 01
Proprietary Data
Millions of scored real-work submissions, rubric-tagged. The only corpus of its kind. Cannot be scraped. Cannot be bought. Must be earned in real time.
MOAT 02
Two-Sided Network
Every challenge brings candidates. Every candidate brings discoverability. Every cert shared brings employers. Standard network effects, but the artifact is the signal itself.
MOAT 03
Scoring Standard
Once "AIQ score" enters the language — once recruiters ask for it — it becomes infrastructure. The first credible standard becomes the only standard.
MOAT 04
Distribution Lock-in
Once embedded in 3 major ATS systems, switching cost is institutional. The Signal API becomes load-bearing for hiring workflows — invisible and irreplaceable.

What kills this · how we defend

The four risks that matter — and our pre-built answers.

Every billion-dollar pitch glosses over the failure modes. Here are ours, named honestly, with the defenses we'll build in advance.

RISK 01 · COLD START
Two-sided marketplace problem. Employers won't post without candidates. Candidates won't show up without employers. The classic chicken-and-egg that kills hiring marketplaces.
DEFENSE
Solve the employer side first with 10 free lighthouse challenges with marquee brands. The brand pulls candidates. The candidates create the artifact library. Six months of pure data investment before monetisation.
RISK 02 · LINKEDIN BUILDS THIS
Microsoft has the distribution, the data, and the capital. If they decide capability scoring matters, they ship it next quarter and we're irrelevant.
DEFENSE
LinkedIn cannibalises its own resume-based business by doing this. They won't. By the time they care, we have the dataset and the standard. The acquisition conversation favours us — they buy us, not build us.
RISK 03 · SCORING CREDIBILITY
If the AIQ score is seen as gameable, biased, or arbitrary, the whole platform collapses. The product is trust in the score.
DEFENSE
Publish the rubric openly. Third-party audit each year. Process trail requirement makes gaming detectable. Recruit an academic advisory board (IO psych, ML fairness) by month 6.
RISK 04 · ENTERPRISE SALES BURNS US
Act 2 requires enterprise sales motion we don't have. Long cycles, RFPs, procurement. The wrong hire kills the company by burning runway.
DEFENSE
Don't sell Act 2 until Act 1 is generating $25M ARR. By then we hire from a position of strength, with a story and a dataset, not a pitch. Wedge first via ATS partnerships, not direct enterprise.
